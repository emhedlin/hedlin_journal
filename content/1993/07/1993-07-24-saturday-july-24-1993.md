We’re nearly half-way through summer holidays. We spent the afternoon on the riverbank watching Formula 1 and SST speedboat races. It was hot. The race preparations were interminable. The river is high and fast, which demands that extra precautions be taken:  guard boats and ambulances are set in place, buoys are fixed, damaged boats are hauled to shore, cranes lower fresh boats into the water. The crowd is in constant motion, everyone trying to improve their view, or going to the concession stand or the bathroom, crawling up the steep bank with two beers in one hand, people in lawn chairs calling their kids out of the bushes.

The boys are changing, quickly. Erik is almost seven. He has stretched out. He has a gap in his smile, with the two permanent teeth starting to show. He has spent most of summer with Jordy Trifa playing soccer in the park, biking around the block, shooting guns, playing Sega, arguing, telling stories meant to test each other’s credulity, helping Jordy’s dad, Avram, build a fence. Jordy is just now in Edmonton to watch Canada’s team play Australia in a World Cup qualifying match. (I hope it’s on television.) Now he spends time alone or with his brothers. He finds some place to lie on the floor, singing, whispering in different voices, playing with cars or drawing Mutant turtles. He still touches his Mom, and praises her beauty.  Cheryl knows this stage is just about over, and has sad eyes and longing smiles while he caresses her arm or leg. At the family reunion in Outlook he was with Graeme playing basketball. He bounces the ball two steps out of every five, and when he crosses the foul line he takes it in hand, runs six or seven steps ahead of the layup. As Matthew puts it, Erik is ‘restless’. When his fits of energy strike, he runs up and down the stairs, races his bike up and down the street, gets on his cleats and kicks his ball against the school, and finds ways to annoy his brothers.

We went to Calgary to attend Eric’s baptism. While there we went hiking in the Valley of the Ten Peaks, near Lake Marraine. Carol and Cheryl walked part of the way. The rest of us, Cameron, Peter, Matthew, Les, Graham, Michael, Erik and I, carried on to Sentinel Pass. We crossed a mountain meadow before climbing to the snow at the top. I began having serious misgivings about having brought Erik. He was tired, the switchbacking path was narrow. I told myself we’d turn back as soon as the hike became too dangerous. Hikers on their way down said, ‘It’s slippery but worth it.’ We carried on. I kept Erik on the slope-side, and switched hands at every turn. Had he slipped from my grip, he wouldn’t have stopped for several hundred meters of large rocks. Once we gained the top, a heavy wind coming up from the Paradise Valley side threatened to make Erik into a kite. We debated whether to go on or return to the bottom. Since the first 50 meters of the path ahead an almost sheer drop, Michael went alone. On the way back I asked Erik how he felt about having done this. He said that this hike was better than sleeping in, better than a movie, better even than having girlfriends.

Matthew is as happy by himself as he is with friends. He is excited when someone calls, Ben, Josh, Matt Johnson, to play soccer or tennis or drive golfballs or run in the field across the street. Most of the time he is alone. When he is on detective cases, he dones a suit jacket laden with detective tools: handcuffs, notepaper, ID card, gun, microphone. He finds clues, types down information. He is stealthy. Sometimes the work takes him to the basement.   Sometimes he is in his bedroom. Or he goes through his stock of toys for things that might be transformed into something else. He practises cello 15 minutes a day, getting ready for the Grambo reunion next weekend.

Peter has shot up to nearly 5’6”. Most of the growth has been in his legs. But one sees gains in the torso, especially as it turns through his golf swing, his elbows high, his back bent, a foot tipped onto the toe. He is fifteen, self-absorbed, aloof. He spends a lot of time in his bedroom reading Elfstone of Shinarra or listening to his new $252 Sony Discman (this he argued long and hard for after he listened to Taylor’s, and no amount of talking by us made a difference. He himself was in long agonized silences, arguing the pros and cons. I think that was partly an attempt to reveal a tortured soul, gain our sympathy, have us pack up our resistance, and tell him, urge him, that if he buys it, it’s okay with us. It was only a matter of time before he pulled together his money and did the deed. He is golfing almost every day.  We bought him a 5-wood which he uses on all his tee-shots and most of the longer fairway drives. His best drives are up, straight and long. He is looking for more consistency on the shorter lengths and the green. But who am I to criticize or praise? I’d be better off playing marbles than swinging a club. Peter’s voice is changing. He is anticipating how low it will get, and that’s where he spends most of his time. He mows the lawn for us and a neighbour. Idle time sits heavy on his hands. He is much happier when things are structured. Give him something to do, say what his timeline is, and he’s never happier. One task keeping him busy for hours is helping Papa on the computer writing the Grambo family history.

Cheryl and I are reading more this summer. Cheryl has read Billy Bathgate, Norma-Jean, Norman Mailer, Skin of the Lion by Michael Odjaate. I read Amis’ Lucky Jim, Showdown by Jorge Amado, Dickens, Porter’s Pale Horse, Pale Rider. Cheryl has finalized arrangements for her transfer from the Admin Masters to the Ed Psych Masters. She is elated ever since her conversation with the head of the department. She will take a class this winter in Stats and apply for an Ed Leave for next year. The head is very happy with her transfer. Very few women enter this field. He asked her to consider a Phd, with the aim of getting on faculty at some point.

I write some, more than I have been. I put off working on the fence. It has remained in its skeletal form ever since Harley Johnson dug the holes and dropped in the poles. His fence is complete on all sides but ours. I am busy putting in grass and exposed-aggregate paving stones along the north side of the house. I would like to be playing more tennis. I am golfing more than I’d have ever dreamed. Matthew and I rode bikes to the river and returned in a downpour. Cheryl was horrified by the mud on our clothes and told us that we had a lot of scrubbing to do if we ever wanted to get the shirts clean.

Cheryl and I are noticing that people our age are beginning to look older than us.
