The French family reunion ended today. Yesterday at 8:30, after several mornings of Matthew asking if it was tomorrow that we were going, we left for Regina. The rain started at Davidson and poured until early Sunday. Water was heavy on the highway. We checked into the Sandman Inn, carried our bags (and sundries) to Room 308, then received notification that eating was happening in the Heartlands Restaurant. There we found Diane and Ernie, Matthew and Cheryl. Their kids together were kicking up a real storm. They were dumping water onto trays and the floor, which they drained back into the cups. They spat mouthfuls of coffee onto plates, picked and pulled the loose floor mouldings, and crawled under the tables, ours and others. Cheryl and I decided to let the others drive to Weyburn, we’d stay back for a nap. We shut off the lights, closed the drapes. Cheryl lay down with Erik and Matthew. Peter, who was not tired, sat in a chair by the window until I took him to the pool, where he waded and dunked. It wasn’t long before Cheryl and Matthew arrived. Matthew was rosy from his sleep. He entered the pool tentatively. We saw Dad and invited him to have coffee with us in the café. Mom had stayed in her room. She is still recovering from the surgery. He sat with us for an hour. A BBQ at cousin Ed’s place is set for 5. We got the boys out of the water in time for them to dry and dress, then drove north to 2727 Partridge Crescent for hamburgers and beans, potato salad, new carrots and dessert. We met some relatives for the first time. Peter and Matthew played all evening with Danny Fredeen’s children, Chris and Erin Zayak, Shirley’s daughter Mvawnway (Muffin), and Kathy from Colorado. When they weren’t laughing at one thing or another in the garage, they were pulling each other through the crowd in the house. Erin and Muffin poked Matthew everywhere to see if he was ticklish. We tried unsuccessfully to talk Carol and Les into staying the night, to swim and watch a movie. We went back to the motel at 10 and put the boys to bed. Erik refused to lie down in the playpen. We sat in the dark and rocked him. By 11 Cheryl and I gave up any hope of reading, put the sleeping boy in the crib and went to bed.

Erik woke at 5, joined us in bed and slept until 7:30. Dad phoned to invite us down for breakfast. We got down by 8:30, helped ourselves to pancakes and cold cereal and coffee. We were joined by Uncle Hartley and Aunt Margaret, Aunt Muriel Welling, and eventually by her daughter Lynn and her boyfriend Bruce. The boys went back to the pool, swam until 10. We were packed and checked out 20 minutes later. I gassed up at Petrocan and arrived at the Franciscan retreat at Lumsden at 11. Cheryl spent several minutes turning the car inside out looking for a wrist chain she had caught and snapped on the door. We then went in. We found a circular brick chapel, a row of easy chairs lined up by the large fireplace, and the cafeteria where everyone was lining up at tables with fruit, pancakes, sausage, ham, butter, coffee, and Saskatoon berry pie with ice-cream. Brother Matthew and Cheryl sat at our table. Peter did not eat. Matthew sat at a table with a boy his age with no name or known association. When the meal was finished we went to another room where Hartley and Howard were showing and narrating their canoeing adventure films. Erik got up the steps to the projector, which (the projector, not Erik) we rescued time and again. He wouldn’t be diverted, so I took him to the lobby with a view of the valley and a corral at the bottom of the hill. I chatted with Aunt Joan Fredeen while Matthew, Peter, Cathy and Muffin played. Many had gone outside to kick balls and throw Frisbees. The rain was over, the sky had cleared, the sun was brilliant, the breeze was light.  Erik squatted on his knees and made sounds at whoever came near.  People began leaving.  Many had a ten-hour trip back to Calgary or Edmonton. Some are flying out of the Regina airport tomorrow to homes in Seattle and Vancouver. After Matthew was done making promises with Muffin to visit, and providing her with phony home addresses and phone numbers, we left. We spent the $2 Aunt Margaret had given the boys at a confectionary on James Street. Once on the road, we didn’t stop. The boys slept the whole way. Erik woke briefly when I got coffee in Chamberlain. Matthew’s last words before falling asleep were, ‘Dad, I’m going to ask Muffin to come to Christopher Lake. Okay?’
