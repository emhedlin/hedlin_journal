Thursday was spent at the Exhibition. It has been rainy. We were lucky to pick the one day that was all sun. We went at 2, found a parking spot, paid the $10 admission, and went to every free event on offer. The first was Superdogs, an oval area with a track in the middle. Matthew was one of three kids picked to open the show. They were to march behind the Emcee with stiff leashes for their invisible dogs. Wearing yellow ‘I Love No-Name’ caps, they sat on a special podium at one end of the track. We walked among the rides, watched people trying to flip rubber chickens into rotating buckets or shoot water into the mouths of plastic clowns in a race with others to be the first to pop their balloon. Stunt men tossed each other around a western-era stage. We went into the Wheatland Building to see the photography and art entries and science displays. We sat for stage performances by ‘Pepper’ the mime and the Walters family. We sat in the Open Air theatre for 30 minutes of a Saskatchewan-Talent performance. We had a meal at Cyrils’, snacked on candy floss and spudnuts, and were back in the car by 7. We dropped by my parents’ and arranged to take them to the airport the following day at noon.

Friday night was spent at Cheryl’s grandparents. Harvey, Anne and David were there with Rose for raspberry shortcake. Peter and Matthew stayed overnight.

Today we were on the road with Cheryl’s parents. We drove to Viscount where Papa reminisced, told us the story about a man we know, Herman Austensen. They had grown up together in Viscount. Herman has always been very careful with his money. One day way back when they were boys, walking together in Viscount on a hot day, Sylvan asked Herman if he’d like an ice-cream cone. Herman answered that he’d prefer the nickel. We drove to the homestead and explored inside and around the farm house. It has been badly chewed up by raccoons. Cheryl’s Dad pointed out where the original building had been added to, and where that addition had been added to. We found the swings, the arched gateway, and a fountain. Lilac bushes have grown against the house, the grass is waist-high. We drove to Viscount beach on Lake Manitou. Papa, the boys and I walked down to the shore. Peter and Matthew waded and skipped stones. Matthew splashed water onto his face and wore salt-stains the rest of the day. We ate BBQed hamburgers and cinnamon buns, listened to the cattle on the far shore, watched teenagers swimming, screaming, throwing each other in and splashing around on a tractor tire tube. We drove around the lake to Manitou Beach. We were rather surprised by how this old place has kept up the ‘glory’ of the ‘50s. The shore is crowded with sunbathers, courts are busy with tennis games, people are strolling along all the streets. There is even a viable massage parlour for ‘people suffering from headaches or nervousness.’ We headed up to Watrous. We parked. I put Erik on my back and walked while he slept. We drove around the town before heading back to Saskatoon. Almost everyone slept on the road. We had supper at the Cavalier. Peter had somehow gotten tar on his shoes, which ended up on his chair. We talked about the possibility of a trip while the boys played and ate. Peter had had too much sun and wasn’t feeling great. A big tent is in the park. The Nightcap Players are performing Macbeth there. An actor was doing pre-performance stretches on the grass. We went home for raspberries and cream. Erik is thrilled he is now free to move. He helped me unload the dishwasher. He picks out the plates and hands them to me, one at a time. Peter grieved over the dying farmhouse. Matthew sat on the curb and floated things in the gutter. Erik floated in bathwater, his head back to submerge his ears, and called and called. Matthew joined him. Peter sat on the sofa reading The Mystery of the Flaming Footprints. He was in bed by 10, coughing from time to time. Matthew is asleep. Cheryl’s chair is set against the stereo to keep Erik away. She’s reading Fortune magazine.
