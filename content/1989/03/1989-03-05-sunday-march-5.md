Peter and Matthew had cello Master Classes this afternoon in Room 1036 of the Education building. Nuttall came from Edmonton to work with cellists, the Mauers came from Colorado for the violins. Though there were plenty of things planned before and after this class, going from early Saturday morning to late evening, and early Sunday until 3:30, we had time only for this one thing.

The winter weather thus far has been cold. We’re feeling the creep of cabin fever. Tempers are short, the boys are aimless and fretful, antagonistic and hyper energetic. If we could get them outside to play for 5 to 6 hours, that would be something. Especially Matthew and Erik. Peter, at least, gets his 10 hours per week of gym. Cheryl and I get our long daily walks, and, since January, badminton at the Lakeview gym Mondays from 9 to 10:30 pm. We were playing until I cracked a rib hitting a brick wall in a floor-hockey game at school. But even this exercise seems insufficient for two adults getting on in years dealing with weight, personality change, altering perceptions that bring more intense moods, such as growing anger over the increase of general social inequalities, and other things so subtle it is easily not noticed until something happens. We got brief respite from our gloominess during a visit to Grandma Skarsgard’s, who is staying in a room in at the Lutheran Sunset Home following a lengthy stay at City Hospital to recover from a stomach operation. Her room is cheery, her mood is light, she was upright and fully clothed on her bed. Cheryl’s Dad sat in the blind-side corner stealing a few minutes of sleep while the boys sat in the wheelchair and made too much noise. I spent fifteen minutes with my Great Uncle Leslie Neatby. He is also at the Sunset Home. He seems grateful for conversation of any sort. His memory still seems good. I introduced myself—reminding him who I was—and he asked how Cheryl was.

Erik continues with his super-abundance of energy, his good nature, his talk, which is part and parcel of the impulse for laughs: an occasional pseudo-prodigious phrase, mimicry, expressions of affection and sweetness: it’s no wonder countless children are born 3 or 4 years apart. By the time a child is 54 months (or thereabouts), we’ve forgotten the inconveniences and stresses pursuant to conception and say, ‘Why not have another?’ But they are wise who don’t quite forget. By ‘they’ I mean women. Vasectomies would not be a thing if not for women. Human history as we know it wouldn’t have been a thing if the Tree of Knowledge had been sterile.

We’re attending a ski-weekend at Camp Kinasao next Friday. Several Redeemer families will attend.

Cheryl’s parents are going to Mexico and California for three weeks. After a week at home, they’ll be off again to Vancouver for another ten days.

More of Cheryl’s family has moved there. Now it’s Darrel and Joan, Cathy and Bruce.

Peter has a gym competition at the end of this month, and a Festival competition a month later.
