We have just returned from Christopher Lake after six nights. We went up on Friday to get Peter from camp. He enjoyed it to the limit. He told stories about ‘Wildman’, the team-effort obstacle course, about Darla Wendland, about Grant the muscle-bound comic-collecting counsellor, the monk’s supper, campfire skits, etc. We moved everything directly into the Peters’ cabin, then went into the lake to wash off the sweat we’d built up during the hot drive up. Cheryl’s parents arrived after supper. From that point on we had a relaxing routine, reading in the mornings, a pick-up breakfast and lunch, sunning on the beach and dock, canoeing with Cheryl or Matthew or Erik or Peter or everyone to Bell’s Beach and back, sailing with Ed’s boat in rough waters, and even in a rough squall that snapped the sail, tore the rope from my hands, and nearly overturned the boat before pushing us at a boiling rate all the way across to Clearsand Beach. Matthew was in tears. Peter held him in his arms and consoled him with ‘Honey, Honey.’ Both were chilled and shaking while I tied down the sail, then went into a cabin at the owner’s invitation to phone the Anderson’s cabin next door to Adolph to ask Cheryl to come with the car. We went to the Trading Post for ice-cream. Cheryl and I walked towards Bell’s Beach along the road behind the cabins. Erik played for long stretches in the living room with his Garfield skateboarders. Peter and Matthew moved about in the water gathering minnows with an orange bucket. We found a caterpillar as thick and long as my index finger, luminous green with tufts of hair along its back, growing out of reddish globs, each tipped with the tiniest diamond. Erik let it go. Peter and I canoed across the lake and back, then around the island. We faced a stiff wind coming back that buffeted the canoe and made it hard to manage. Tuesday evening we drove to PA, walked a few blocks with Erik trailing most of the way with a heavy stick, whacking trees, rocks, curbs, then running to catch up. We went to Digger’s for coffee, drinks, mud pie and sundaes.

We returned to Saskatoon Thursday at noon, cleaned up the house and yard, then began organizing everything for the garage sale at Cheryl’s parents. We took junk to the dump, hauled sale items to 833 East Centre, set up the tables, laid everything out, hung up the clothes, attached price tags to everything. Saturday began early. People came in waves all morning. That’s when most sales were made. We netted $533. Most people wandered around, looking at things, not buying. I considered going from an unstated policy of ‘Touch it, it’s yours’ to ‘Look at it, it’s yours’. The day finished with pizza at Venice’s on Central Avenue. Cheryl and I turned in at 9:30. The physical part of the day wasn’t so much, but we were still drained.

This morning Erik showed up at our bed, only to find Cheryl missing. She was having a bath. He pushed his hand under the blankets on her side so just the tip of his hand showed and said, ‘Would you at least rub my finger?’

We’re going to visit the Summer Games Plaza to see the variety of things we’ve been hearing so much about. Fireworks at 9:30.
