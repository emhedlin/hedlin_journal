Erik and Matthew are with Mama and Papa at Birch Hills. They headed away after Matthew’s practise and game with the Saskatoon Summer Game’s soccer team. They’re planning to meet a family of Norwegian Bergs. Two of their children are near Matthew and Erik in age.

Erik is stretching up. He has long feet. He broke his big left toe at Northey’s. He was kicking a ball. During the follow-through he jammed his bare foot into a wall. While examining the injury, Orin gave it a firm (painful) squeeze and asked, ‘Is your toe always so crooked.’

Erik was in tears while Cheryl gave it a more tender assessment. He’s lucky the injury came at the end of the soccer season. He would have been crushed to miss playing on the Cosmos, a team he loves.

We had a rather dramatic season-end barbecue at Crocus Park a couple of weeks ago. It was such a steaming-hot day, I feared no-one would show up. I pushed our wheeled barbecue across the street to the school ground. We pulled together a couple of tables. By then the parents and kids bad begun arriving. Hardly had the last of them shown up when the sun disappeared behind heavy dark clouds. Someone wondered if this would be a short event. I rushed through the awards ceremony, handing out one for each of the players: Alex, Trevor, Corey, Tim, Erik, Geordie, Curtis, Scott, David, Justin, Brett, Avrim and Janet Trifa, Brian Boyes. The cooking and eating got underway. The clouds, deep blue by now, were roiling and firing off brilliant bolts of lightning. With some relief we observed their movement to the north east, into Mennonite country. Someone made the cracks about it being better for Aberdeen than us. After eating, while the women visited, the men and boys played a game of soccer. Matthew joined. Erik was willing to risk getting his toe stomped. The men tired, the game turned into a rugby match, and one by one they rejoined the women. By then the clouds had turned purple and were moving southward. I predicted that in ten minutes it would start to rain. The words weren’t out of my mouth when the wind came up. Jovial mood turned to panic. Everyone began grabbing their things and running. Erik picked up a lawn chair. Cheryl and Matthew got the food, tablecloths and chairs. I ran with the barbecue, the red cooler sitting on top, a turkey burger, three hotdogs and two hamburgers still cooking inside. A gust of wind tore the lid off the cooler. Erik’s chair had turned into a sail. He was lifting off the ground. Trees were bending, garbage was blowing everywhere, sand stung our eyes. Kelly from the corner house ran to me and grabbed the cooler. Once inside, we watched through the front window. A car sped around the corner and pulled in front of Classens next door. As it stopped, our garbage bin crashed into its rear end. The tip of our young linden was whipping at the ground, shingles were tearing off the roofs across the street. Erik found this thrilling. Then the power went out. As quickly as the storm came, it ended. The sun broke through, the wind died. We went for a walk to survey the damage. St. Luke’s skylight had been blown off and smashed on the school’s lawn. Patches of roofs along Emmeline were stripped to the plywood. A contractor’s truck paused near us. He asked if we wanted an estimate. Up and down the bock families were out on the street to see what they had lost. Harley next door noticed that the school’s flag was pointing south while the clouds were sailing north. Behind them were more heavy clouds flashing with lightning. The storm was not over. We went back inside and watched the most dramatic lightning I can remember: strobe flashes, lightning bolts, walls of light. It continued for an hour. Peter arrived from work. He had heard on the radio that plough winds were 125 kph. Big trees and entire fences and roofs across the city had been blown apart. Curtis contacted Erik. His entire family had hidden in their basement. Curtis had never been so scared in his life. Mama and Papa drove around taking pictures of the damage. For the next two days people were telling their second- and first-hand accounts of destruction from Radisson out west and northeast beyond Osler: silos, granaries, roofs, barns, grain trucks wrecked, the debris flying hundreds of yards.

While in town Erik and Geordie are constantly together. If they’re not in our back yard playing soccer, they’re sitting under the Green Ash talking about what to do. They lounge on the front yard, for a change, and ride bikes in the park, play tennis with a worn-out ball, and play Clue, Monopoly or Cathedral in the family room. Michael Classen from next door and a girl from down the street have been at our door, asking Erik to play.

Summer bliss.

Matthew is playing a lot of soccer although he isn't spending much time on the recommended fitness schedule. He bought an electric bass guitar from Mother’s music with a set of headphones. All we hear is the occasional buzz of a string. He wants to be ready for jazz band when school begins. He is reading James Herriot.

We were at Christopher Lake. Matthew, Erik and I went for a late evening stroll following the lake shore a couple of days ago. We were waiting for the couple sitting on the dock to leave. Once they were gone we went onto the dock and watched the clouds. One in particular got our attention. It was a half-circle, the shape of a barrel ring. Matthew said he wished he had a girlfriend and they could sit on the dock in the evening with clouds blowing overhead just like they were. He pointed in the direction of the couple now gone from sight. A couple of loons were bobbing in the dark water. I made my own loon call. I asked Cheryl later, who had been in the trailer at the time, if she had heard me. She pretended she hadn’t and that I was silly to think she could.

We shopped for a bike helmet for Matthew’s Grade 8 grad. We picked a gold Bell Image for over a hundred dollars. Matthew wore it in the van on the way home and said what a sweet helmet it is. We bought Peter a fleece for his Grade 12 grad.

Peter is working full-time for the city at Harry Bailey pool as an instructor and a life-guard. He is taking a bit of time off from the quartet since returning from UVic where he spent the year studying with the Lafayette quartet, a quartet from Mexico city, and a cellist with the Cleveland quartet. Every day, he tries to make the most fun of everything. He plays Magic with Derek, who wears a fedora and talks in grumbling tones, he golfs with Alan at Wildwood (the plan to play 18 holes was halved because Alan had to go home for supper). He goes with Sarah Woods to hear a line-up of bands on 21st street. He told Cheryl that we should meet her sometime. He said she was different from the other girls because she had many interesting things to say. She was very busy working weeks at the Bible Society and weekends as a recreational coordinator at one of the paddling pools.

He has been shopping for a single-lens reflex camera. He reads whatever camera-literature he can get his hands on, visits camera stores to talk to salespeople, and updates us on what he has his eye on. He has perfected his mimicry of the Sikh taxi-driver in Victoria (which makes Papa nearly burst with laughter) and the Schwann’s ice cream travelling salesman who came to our house some time ago. He has a role in play for Folkfest. His character is the understudy of a schoolmaster who is holier-than-thou and a bit of a sneak. He is planning to go camping with Michael Barth. The SYO kids are going to Christopher for a weekend at the end of July. He is submitting an availability form to the City for work this fall.
