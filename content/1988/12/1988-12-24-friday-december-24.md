We are in the middle of Christmas holidays. The boys are presently watching Mr. Dressup. Peter is waiting for Cheryl to be done a phone-call to her mother about gifts they’re putting together for a family on Avenue P, and I’m waiting for Cheryl to get the car out of the driveway so I can shovel the foot of snow that fell through yesterday. The only snow we’ve had so far.

We’re busy getting ready for Christmas I can never believe the amount of detail involved in these occasions. There will be no peace until 6:30 tomorrow night when supper is served. Cheryl and Peter are off to ToyCity. We’ve taken charge of a family. We delivered a food hamper yesterday. The mother of these children sat on her porch seeming bewildered by all the boxes and bags building up around her. We’ll deliver clothing and gifts today. Peter and Erik helped haul things while Matthew slept in the front seat.

The past few months have brought many changes in Erik. This is due partly to the natural growth of unique qualities, partly to his brothers’ influences. He is active, highly interactive, humour loving. He trails his brothers everywhere, plays with their toys with or without their agreement. And when not, we hear sudden outbursts: ‘Erik! Please give it back! Mom, Erik has my car!’ Moments later Erik races past us. Often he has to be talked out from under his bed. He attacks them from behind with a shriek and a choke-hold, pull them over, instigates a body-roll, while the brothers gasp for breath or laugh. And then peace again.

Erik talks freely, fully, tells us whatever he desires. His older, gentle, tolerant brothers have given in to him enough to make him extremely headstrong. Sleep ends for us every Saturday and Sunday morning around 8 when sounds start up in his room, then our door bangs open, the crown of his head passes by the foot of our bed, where he stands like a cowboy who has entered the saloon, and waits until we call him in. He climbs up and strokes Cheryl’s face and rubs his feet against her legs.

His regular routine is to sleep until 7:15, get us up, lie in bed while Cheryl prepares in our little bathroom and I shower and shave in the basement. He eats from our bowls at breakfast and is fed a second breakfast of porridge when Bev or Darlene find the time. He plays, or may watch Mr. Dress-up or Sesame Street, talking along with the TV, repeating letters, numbers, or French words. He has a large lunch of peanut butter and jam sandwiches cut on the diagonal, or warmed-up suppers like spaghetti and meat-sauce. At 1 he sleeps. This he loves to do, smiling as he lies down, pulling his blankets to his face, locating a toy, talking and singing for a quarter of an hour, kicking the wall, picking at the wallpaper seams, sometimes standing at the window and playing with the blinds, pulling out the wood slats and whacking the retaining bar still attached from the old days when it served as the upper bunk. He sleeps for 1½ hours, then shows up in the hallway when he’s done, most often in the best mood. He plays, or is read to. He doesn’t sit on a chair. He prefers the corner of the sofa, and leans against the chair to look down on the pictures. He persists in this good mood until the boys arrive home from school. He might watch TV with them or wrestle or play in the basement if that’s where they choose to go. By 5 he becomes aggressive, loud, whiney, and stays that way until he’s had his first mouthfuls of food: hunger aggravates him exactly as it did the other boys at this age. When supper is over, Cheryl and I spend time with him doing whatever comes to mind. Peter and Matthew are in bed before Erik. By 9 or 9:30 we undress him, share affections, play in the living room, then put him to bed. He insists on prayers being said. He repeats them line by line to the end. He takes awhile to settle down, never fussing, except if he wants a drink, which he takes sitting amidst the turmoil of his bedding. Then he’s quiet.
