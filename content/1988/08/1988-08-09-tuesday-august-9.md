The day after arriving in Sheridan, we made our way slowly to Cody. The first 60 miles through Big Horn National Forest brought us through spectacular climbs to an elevation of 8500 feet. The grade at times became so steep we were reduced to 45 mph. While stopping at the Visitor’s Centre to look into the deep river gorge, we noticed heavy black smoke coming from beyond the next bend. Given the dryness, we assumed part of the forest was on fire. We didn’t expect to find an RV in full flame. Waiting with the rest of the traffic, within 45 minutes it had collapsed, with only blackened framework remaining. The owners were up the road, standing amidst the curious. Park workers were above the RV in the trees creating a firebreak.

We ate lunch in Greybull by the Bighorn River. We shopped for groceries and enjoyed the luxury of air-conditioned respite from the hot car. We found a city park, set the food on blankets, and ate while the boys played on swings and painted tractor tires. They wanted to stay longer.

We got only as far as Cody. By 4 it seemed to late to try to drive to Yellowstone. The boys have had enough driving. We made the decision to stay while eating ice-cream at the Dairy Queen. Cheryl and I went looking for a campground, located the Gateway Campsite, set up our tent, then joined her parents for supper at Buffalo Bill’s Irma Hotel, named for his daughter. It was a unique place with high tin-pressed ceilings and cherry wood bar, built in 1902 for $100,000. Erik was fussy, so I took him for a walk up and down the street past stores full of western-style products. We washed our clothes at the Wild West Laundromat, bought rodeo tickets, left Erik with Mama and Papa at their hotel, and made it to the stadium just west of Cody in time for opening ceremonies. We sat in the Buzzard’s Roost and watched bareback and saddle broncos, bull riding, calf roping, team calf-roping (a neat maneuvering to catch the animal’s hind foot) and barrel racing, with brief comic breaks provided by 2 trained dogs, pole-climbing clowns, and our own son, Matthew, who along with other children tried his luck on the back of a galloping sheep.

We spent time in a futile search for my remaining Travellers’ cheques, visited the Trading Post where Matthew and Peter picked up some sheriff badges, gassed up, and left by way of the Shoshone Canyon, past the Buffalo Bill Reservoir along a deep canyon drive, through a tunnel, to Bill Cody’s hunting lodge outside Yellowstone Park east entrance. We ate lunch in the dining room. Erik went several times to the souvenir shop to hug every stuffed toy in reach, including bear-paw slippers. He pointed at the mounted moose head and said ‘cow?’ We entered the park at 1, followed Lake Yellowstone, saw where the forest fires were still active. Provisional water pipes crossed the road, bringing water where it’s needed. We were stopped at one point, told to keep our headlights on and not stop. We reached Old Faithful by 4, watched a film, walked to the thermal holes, and returned to the geyser in time for its 4:50 eruption. Erik sat on my shoulders most of the way and asked about things with a hushed voice. His patience wore thin when Old Faithful was delayed. Mama bought us double-dip ice-cream cones. We drove away along a highway with a view of spectacular falls, fields of massive boulders, herds of elk, lines of fishermen working the river, into Mammoth Hot Springs (a tidy Cedar-built government town) where we got our last look at the park, then down to Montana. We ate supper in an expensive restaurant with a beautiful view of the southern hills, then followed the Yellowstone river valley up Highway 89 to Livingston and onto the I-90 to Boseman.

We visited the Lewis and Clark Caverns in Montana. Mama stayed with Erik in the parking lot while we joined a tour and climbed a steep slope for ¾ miles to a man-made mouth into the caverns. Papa had a tough time. Matthew stopped half-way then rode on my back the rest of the way. At one point, with his arms in a strangle-hold around my neck, he said ‘Dad, boy am I sweating.’ We took photos of the Jefferson River valley, then dropped into the caverns. The guide began by warning that we mustn’t touch the stalagmites and stalactites. The natural oil on our hands will affect their growth.

Shortly, Matthew asked, ‘Dad, what did she say about growth?’

A bit later he asked Cheryl, ‘Mom, how tall am I?’

She said, ‘Well, I don’t think you’ve grown much since you came in here.’

He looked worried.

We visited the Twilight room, Cathedral room, and so on, following steep, long, sharp-turning stairways. The guide spoke of pack-rat urine trails, bats, pigeons, the worker who in the ‘30s, stole into the caverns to take things he thought he might sell to raise money for his fare back home and was lost for two days in the pitch black. He told also about another worker who fell from the spiral staircase into a 90 foot hole, about the ribbon rock (‘cave bacon’), popcorn, the Fantasy Room with its stone shadows. We descended the Bear’s Slide, hit the ‘speed bumps’, went through the Chinese Back Scratchers and the ‘Head-knockers’ (where Cheryl grabbed the arm of a small girl who was sitting at the edge of the black hole where the worker had died). The temperature in the caverns were 50 F year-round. They were discovered in the 1880s by a rancher who had watched steam rising from the mountain. Peter was so excited by the descent past damp walls and cellar-like staircases he said he would love to do it on his own.

We drove straight to Helena before eating at Frontier Pies. Erik slept the whole way. Add that to the hour he slept in the parking lot, that means a stretch of 3 ½ hours. After lunch we drove to Great Falls. The highway along the Dearborn and Missouri rivers is as scenic as anything. We checked with the KOA, found the $20/night price to be exorbitant, and opted to stay at the Fox Hollow Inn with Cheryl’s parents, a two-bedroom condo behind the Heritage Inn on 10th Avenue. We watched an old Hollywood Channel, The Monkees and Car 54 Where Are You?

There was shopping at Target in the Village Inn Mall for clothes and shoes and pots and pans for Lloyd, then 4Bs for lunch.

Matthew’s version of ‘I’ve Been Working on the Railroad’ ends with ‘And scrubbin’ on the ol’ banjo.’

The boys bought a ‘safe dartboard’ and played in the living room while Erik slept. Dennis the Menace was on TV.

While in the mall Erik sits in his umbrella stroller, then gets out and pushes it with zig-zags and wheelies, nearly taking out displays and legs, refusing to let go. We visit bookstores and toy shops while Cheryl looks for pants for the boys.

We have supper at Elmer’s. Erik bit into a cherry tomato and squirted the juice on Mama.

We were in bed early. It’s Canada in the morning. Matthew slept in the closet. After packing and breakfast we were on the road. We had a three-hour stop in Havre while Papa looked for unlined gloves. The roads to Consul were mostly poor. Hitting one particularly sharp bump, two plastic hubcaps were jarred loose, rolled ahead and alongside the car a short distance before veering into the ditches, and history. At about this point we parted company with Cheryl’s parents. We reached Cypress Hills by 4.

We tented amidst beautiful Lodgepole pine at #32 campsite of the Rainbow campground. We roasted marshmallows, the boys played in the woods, ‘thrashing’ stumps with sticks, sword fighting, Erik joining in with a leafy branch. Peter tended the fire. ‘I’m good at this,’ he said, while tossing in wood, building the flame. We visited the swimming pool Thursday afternoon. Lots of shallow areas where Erik splashed and Matthew went gliding, imagining himself a swimmer. Last night he couldn’t sleep because he dreamed he was doing tricks in gymnastics that other kids couldn’t do, which meant being bumped up to the men’s competition. ‘Some’ people were cold during the night. The breeze became cool and even in the bright sunlight the boys got chilled. I took Erik into the hot tub. He smiled as his face slowly became flushed. Peter, Matthew and I went on a short hike in the Windfall valley. Peter gave a couple of trees a hard whack with a stick and said, ‘This is where my head got manufactured.’ Earlier, Peter and Matthew had been arguing about who had the bigger brain.
