Our original plan to stay 2 nights changed. After cereal in the room and coffee in a cafe we formed two groups. We went to the beach, stationed ourselves near the lighthouse. Cheryl gathered stones. Peter, Matthew and Erik soaked themselves in the waves while throwing stones, pushing a log, flinging muddy sand. I snapped a few black and white pictures to finish off the film.

We met Mama and Papa for lunch, triscuits, gouda and string cheese, apple fritters. Then we relocated from the lighthouse to a place on the beach where bigger waves were rolling in. Cheryl read a newspaper near the parking lot. The boys and I played a game of football. Peter runs, gritting his teeth, showing only braces, makes a couple of great catches. Matthew and Erik are opposing quarterbacks. We draw our plays on the sand. Erik’s play was: I run, fake left, fake right, go straight. Peter and Matthew win 21 - 7. Eriks counts this as our second loss, but doesn’t know why this has happened. We take the ball back to Cheryl, then play tag with the surf, holding hands, running out, not letting the foam catch our toes, assigning marks for style. Peter and Matthew do handstands in the rushing surf. ‘Ho,’ says Peter, once he’s back on his feet, ‘is that disorienting.’ They do roundoff back handsprings. Sand and water splashing. Erik’s shorts were rolled up, but they unrolled and the cuffs filled with water. Matthew’s hair hangs in his eyes, but short around the ears.

We leave Crescent City by 2:30, follow the coast, plan to stop for the night at Coos Bay. However, every motel is full. We drive to Reedsport and find the same thing. We phone for a motel in Eugene. (I end up talking with a girl at the central dealing agency to book two rooms at the Econolodge, but she is (she must be) tired and asks which state Oregon is in.)

We follow the Siuslaw river past elk-sighting spots. In the dying light we see them hunkering down in the grass.  We arrive in Springfield north of Eugene. Erik is asleep and needs to be carried to the room. Next morning he says, ‘I don’t remember coming to this hotel.’
