I walked to Safeway in the mist for milk and croissants for breakfast. After we ate we all walked to the Bay and caught the cablecar to the far end of the line, Market and Powell. There, we walked to Nordstrom’s for coffee and danish and cookies. Mama and Papa split off with the plan to meet in 2 hours, 1:30. We rode the curving escalators to the 8th floor past the piano player, then down by elevator and outside onto Market, where a South American music group was set up at the cable car turn-around. They played and danced and sang; beautiful harmonies. We walked to Macy’s where Cheryl bought a dress. The boys and I walked to FAO Schwartz and its three floors of noise, crowds and toys. Matthew and Erik bought Porsche and Ferrari model cars. Outdoors, we strolled past immense hotels, small shops, parks, buskers—one who sang opera—to a discount book store, bought New Yorkers for $3.88, then back to Nordstroms, and met Mama and Papa. Cheryl was quite late in returning. She held a package behind her, smiling. A second dress. We went to the top floor again to the cafe for soup, sandwiches, hotdogs. We split up again. Cheryl, the boys and I crossed Market, found more homeless. We stood in line to buy cable car tickets behind an Asian man who was fumbling the unfamiliar dollar bills into the slot of the ticket machine, but they were being rejected and he didn’t know why. A woman with a red swollen face 3 and a very insistent voice, standing yards distant, was trying to instruct him. ‘Turn it over! Push the button first!…’ Which only made it worse. Clearly he wasn’t understanding. I stepped in to assist, pointing to the money, which he was putting in upside down, then to the button. It went smoothly. He bought 4 tickets. ‘Hey,’ the woman said, ‘You’re taking my job. How am I supposed to make any money.’ Then she smiled. Then she held out a styrofoam cup. I gave her 50 cents. We got into a long line, caught the Beach & Hyde trolley, paused at the midpoint for a change of drivers.

Back at the hotel the boys settled in to play. Cheryl and I went out again, walked to Union, down to Van Ness, back as far as Lombard, followed the sidewalk along the switchback past hydrangea bushes, then down a street so steep our feet pushed into the toes of our shoes, and took a zig-zagging route back to the motel.

Shortly after, Mama and Papa returned. We went for supper to TGI Friday. Hamburgers, grilled chicken, chicken strips, potato skins, Thai chicken with a sauce, served by a 6’9” waiter. Music was loud, the place was full, a voice at the front door calling names of people in line. As we were finishing, the waiter came by and asked if that was everything. We said yes. Erik said to Cheryl, ‘How come they ask the dads about things like dessert and never the kids.’

We headed back to the motel to get the boys ready for bed. Mama and Papa arrived with chocolate for the boys. We decided that Cheryl, Papa and I would take a last ride on the cable cars. From Bay & Beach to Nob Hill, off onto the Nob Hill trolley to the end and back, walked around the Flood Mansion, past huge hotels, through a park with a Roman fountain, back on a trolley, down California to the Financial District, back to Powell and to Market, off again at the turnaround where a tall black man in orange hum suit wandered along the empty line, singing, thrusting out his hand for emphasis, in a deep voice that resonated off walls, with no-one there to give him money. Papa asked, ‘What do you think is wrong with him?’ We caught the last car to the Bay and returned to the motel to bed.
