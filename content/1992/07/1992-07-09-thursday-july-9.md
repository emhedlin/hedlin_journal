Summertime. Matthew is having his last full day at Camp Kinasao. Peter has two days left in his Lifesaving III. Erik being Erik is prowling the neighbourhood in search of someone to play with, practising a little piano for his lesson on Monday, playing with Lego and the Sega Racecar game Peter brought home from our trip to Minot. Cheryl is listening to Dvorak's String Quartets and baking Peter's birthday cake. The exhibition is on but I think we won’t go this year. We have had two days of rain, and water is building up at the back of the backyard.

I returned from Japan on the 1st . Cheryl brought the boys to the airport. I was struck by how much taller they were than my memory of them two weeks previously. Both sets of parents were there. Everyone came over and we visited until 10:30 pm. The days that followed, I scarcely remember. I was tired from the combined effects of two weeks of constant travel and jet lag. I slept compulsively, reminding me of the lingering effects of anaesthetic after two bouts of surgery years earlier.

Peter and Cheryl went to Minot on Saturday afternoon and are due to return on Tuesday. Saturday evening, Matthew, Erik and I went to Kinsmen Park, did all the rides, played soccer in the park, went for ice-cream where Erik and I shared a Blizzard (Erik was insisting that I eat the last part, while saying, ‘Are you sure, dad?’) and Matthew ate a medium dipped cone. Sunday morning I watched the Wimbledon finals, bathed both boys and got Matthew’s suitcase ready for his week at camp. I had been duly warned to remember Matthew’s earplugs: ‘David, I know you're going to forget Matthew's earplugs and then he won't be able to go into the water’. We drove with both Erik and Matthew in the front seat to keep me awake. Matthew talked the whole way about what he was expecting at Christopher Lake. After arriving, we registered in the Camp Center, moved Matthew’s bags to Cabin 9, met his counsellor Lee, then headed down to the lake. Matthew was a little worried that he would miss the opening event, so after a few minutes of distracted play on the beach he told me he should head up. He went with barely a look over his shoulder. Erik took off his shoes and socks and threw stones in the water. Ione and Crystal were returning from a birthday party for Dawn at Ed’s and Shirley’s. We were invited for coffee. Erik stayed outside to throw more stones. Ione told me about the plans to raise the cabin and add a living room. On our way out of Kinasao I spotted Matthew in the chain of campers, the front end pulling the rest around its own centre. Matthew didn’t see us. We waved anyway.

For the rest of the weekend, Erik and I kept busy. I woke up at 9:30 thinking there was plenty of time before the Travellers’ Day parade began, until I discovered it was beginning just then. Erik had been playing on his bed quietly to let me sleep. When he saw I was up, he hurried to get ready. We parked on University Drive and walked down the bridge. We hadn’t missed a thing. The sun was strong when the parade began, but heavy clouds passed over and rain fell on the column of people on the bridge. Erik said he hated being on the bridge. He was afraid it would break apart. After breakfast, we were called by Aunt Margaret Fredeen who asked if Erik would play with Tara, her lively, lonely granddaughter. Erik thought he would be shy, but I was hardly out of the car and he was in the first branch of the tree, talking to his new friend. I played tennis with Jack Nepjuk. Scott Farmer came by and invited us over for coke. We talked about holidays and Japan. When Jack left we talked a few minutes more, but Scott became quiet. I said they were probably going to have supper soon and I should go. He said yes they were, but only tomato sandwiches. I ate a bun for supper and sat down to read Nicholas Nickelby. Erik was dropped off in a heavy thundershower. He raced indoors and watched lightning through the front window. He had had a great time with Tara. They had gotten into some trouble with Uncle Hartley. They were jumping on the bed when Tara fell and bumped her forehead. Uncle Hartley scolded them. Erik said, ‘I think he was correct.’

He plays quietly by himself. He came downstairs in a jean jacket with a rifle under one arm, both barrels cocked, and a fat play-dough knife in an inside pocket.

We watched Rescuers Down Under and ate a large bowl of popcorn. Erik had seen it at Shirley’s but enjoyed it the second time. He talked throughout. It was over at 9:30. I flossed his teeth—he has a large cavity in a lower molar—and he went to bed.

Tuesday, Erik spent much of the day watching for Cheryl and Peter to arrive. I took him to Delphi’s for pizza. He ate slowly, every bite had to be prompted. I said he had two slices, but he ate only one. I cut the second in small pieces, and he ate two. I chatted briefly with Leanne Golding, a former student. Erik asked me about her afterwards. We got milk from Safeway, played baseball in front of St. Lukes (with new rules about hitting singles, triples, fouls, home runs), and finished off watching Sylvester and Tweety Bird cartoons. I spent part of this time transcribing minutes from the last SYO executive meeting.

Cheryl and Peter were home by 9:30. They hauled in more than they had gone with. Papa bought Peter a gel bicycle seat as a souvenir. Cheryl seemed rested. They had found some good places to eat. Peter had spent a lot of time in the pool showing Papa his lifesaving techniques.

While I was away, Erik wrapped up his first season of outdoor soccer. The players received small trophies which Erik is keeping on the piano. Matthew had been playing goal in some tournament games and was strongly complimented by an opposing coach who also plays soccer. I had learned this on a phone call I made from Kyoto. Once home, I learned that Peter has been pitching for his baseball team. He was surprised by how well the game had gone. He hasn’t had any coaching. He has a strong arm.
