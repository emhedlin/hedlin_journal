Christmas holidays. The boys are in the basement playing ping pong. Matthew and Erik got new rackets with tacky surfaces for spin control. Everyone tries very hard not to lose. Losers will face days of gloating and questions that lay doubt on one’s manhood. And we play as long as it takes for someone to be a loser. A fine socialist household.

We have made a special point this Christmas to build anticipation for Christmas Eve and Christmas Day. The Sunday before Christmas was the Sunday school performance. Monday, Cheryl and I took Mark and Nancy Rudoff to John’s. Peter and Matthew babysat Helena and Jackson, who were in bed by 8:30. They spent the rest of the evening, Peter and Matthew, watching a Peter Pan animated movie. Tuesday afternoon and evening was spent with my family, first at the Sheraton Cavalier where Dad bought lunch for Diane’s family, Uncle Hugh and Aunt Vi, and Joel and Michelle. They announced their engagement here. Across Spadina, city workers were flooding the Meewasin rink. They couldn’t know that in a week the Pacific air will bring temperatures to 10+. Peter went home afterwards to study for his last exam and we went to Mom and Dad’s. Uncle Ed, Aunt Audrey and Ernie’s parents were there. The men talked about farming. Uncle Hugh held court on a wide range of topics, from farming to government to Quebec separatism. Aunt Vi looked was her jolly self in spite of the cancer concerns that have dogged her in recent years. Ernie was recently treated for colon and lung cancer. It didn’t come up. He was uncharacteristically talkative and engaging. This is the culminating crisis in a chain events that have coloured family relations for 10 years. We stayed until 9 or so. Wednesday just our family had supper by candlelight and choral music. I took it one step too far by suggesting we take turns reading classic Christmas stories. The boys balked.

Matthew asked, ‘Does 17 mean something to you?’

I asked what he meant.

He said, ‘Seventeen years of age is not when you should spend time with your family.’

Interesting point.

We skipped ahead to watch A Christmas Story. Cheryl had heard great reviews from people at school. The boys Ralphie and Randy are so much like much young Peter and Matthew, to felt like we watched our own home movie. When it ended the boys scrammed. Peter went with Stuart, Derek and Joe to play pool at the Sutherland Bar. Matthew went with Guru, Hoover and whoever else, and Erik went to Curtis’ across the street. Cheryl and I were suddenly alone. With music in the background, we read.

Thursday evening was little Christmas Eve. We planned to invite Cheryl’s parents to see Christmas lights before having appetizers somewhere. Cheryl’s Dad beat us to the draw and invited us to Willow’s for supper. They went ahead while I sat with an internet service technician who was installing software to speed up our internet. Back at home we watched White Christmas. Papa admired the beautiful actresses and chuckled at the patter between Danny Kaye and Bing Crosby. One sees him drifting back to a time when he was young, the film was new, life was simpler, and all those faces were fresh and alive.

On Friday, they came over just after 5 with food and gifts. We put steaming pots and serving dishes of sweet potatoes, white potatoes, carrots, peas, turkey, dressing, cranberry sauce, juice, buns, butter, salads, and the crowning glory, rømmegrøt, in places between candles, china, silverware, napkins. We went to the Candlelight service. Erik accolyted. Trying to reach the wick of the candle he came nearly to knocking it over while the congregation held its breath. The holy family is fortunate Erik hadn’t been their obstetrician. History may have looked very different. On the way home we drove through the Forestry Farm Christmas display. We had Christmas pudding for dessert and opened the gifts. Erik was assigned to hand them out. Will he be relieved of the task when he’s 6 feet and pushing 40? Not if his brothers have a say.

Next morning I was up in the dark. Papa joined me shortly after. He wondered why the boys weren’t already there shaking their gifts. In time they arrived. The women came last. The gifts were opened, we ate oranges, King’s College Choir and Dave Brubeck played in the background. The boys took apart the hard-drive to install a new CD burner. They played ping pong with their new rackets. We watched It’s a Wonderful Life. We ate what was left from yesterday’s meal. We sat in the living room and listened to old-time radio show cassettes that we gave Papa. Papa and Mama joined Cheryl at the TV to watch figure skating. They left at 9 pm.

On Sunday we remained home for most of the day. I walked around with my new tennis racket. Reading broke out all over: Peter with Cleopatra; Erik with the Redwall rat fantasy; Cheryl with‘Tis and some book about healthy cells; me with Napoleon, Robberbride, and Suchet’s Last Master. Cheryl and I went to Costco for food for tomorrow evening. Back home, we found the boys fully dressed in snowboarding gear. The temperature outside was nearly 10+. Matthew has plans to go to Blackstrap mountain on Monday with Hoover and another boy who’s father owns half the mountain. They’re getting free lift passes and snowboard rentals.

Erik bought $80 goggles, $15 off.

They were ‘cool’, Matt said.

Erik said, ‘I’ll have them for the rest of my life.’

I said, ‘I don’t know about that. Wear them at my death bed. Beyond that, who cares.’

A few of us got food ready. Peter rolled meatballs, I clipped skin and ugly bits off chicken wings, and Cheryl did the rest, including arranging napkins on the table with each edge offset by half an inch to make a long paper-made Christmas-looking diamond. The knives were arranged like soldiers on parade, forks were stacked like coffins after a heavy battle, and so on. Peter and I watched Dog Day Afternoon. Matthew is mostly away with his friends in the evening. Hoover, Guru, Kevin and Jason are a travelling caravan that speaks mostly in single syllables.

The phone rings. Matthew gets it.

‘Eh’, he says.

‘Eh’, someone says back.

If Erik answers the phone and it’s Guru on the line, the silliness really gets going. Erik asks if Guru’s jacket is glued on. Whatever Guru answers, Erik goes ‘what, what, what, what’. It takes a flying body-part to stop him.

Papa came by and picked up Erik, Peter and me to go to Compu-smart for CDs for the burner and a computer for Papa. We returned by noon. Preparations began in earnest for the gathering that evening. People began arriving by 5:15: Diane and Ernie (who took a tour because in the 9 years we’ve lived here, they’ve never been), my parents, Cheryl’s parents, Joel, Michelle and her sister Colette. We expected Carol and Les, but they had other plans. We ate in three rooms and the children played pieces: Peter and Matthew on cello, Matthew on his bass (such a big grin for such a little amp), Erik on the piano, the Kardash girls on piano. After each song we lightly applauded. Everyone was gone by 10.

Tuesday, the holiday began in earnest for Cheryl. I’ve been stealing moments for myself every day. The boys have a way of scattering like rats in a sudden light. Erik has been lying on the love seat reading his book with one leg hanging over the back. (He stressed the point that he has read more of this book in 5 days than he read in the Shannara book in two years.)  He has a soccer tournament, with two games on Tuesday, one on Wednesday, one on Thursday. Peter and I went to see Fight Club, an anarchist fantasy with Brad Pitt. Peter and Matthew went to Zion Wednesday evening to be part of an ensemble group that will play for a church service Sunday evening at SaskPlace. Matthew took off early Thursday morning to go snowboarding with Guru. Both times he has taken the Honda. He was thinking of going to Table Mountain, but went to Blackstrap instead. Guru phoned at 7:30. Cheryl took the call, still in a dead sleep, and went to Matthew’s dark room, calling him, searching for him in his blankets, and expressing her shock when she discovered he was already up and dressed and showered and in fine spirits and nearly ready to go. Peter is rifling through a bunch of songs, singing and laughing as he listens, picking the tunes that Papa would most like to listen to. Cheryl is busy sewing. Moments earlier she was on her knees with the pain of banging her knee on the sewing machine cabinet. Besides the gash, was the knee cap broken?
