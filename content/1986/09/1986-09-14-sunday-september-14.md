The routine has returned. Saturday morning was spent at the University with both boys. Matthew has officially begun playing cello. He was unhappy that his group didn’t actually play their instrument. When asked who knew Go Tell Aunt Rhody, Matthew said he did. He added that he knew a lot of other things. Bernadette agreed that Matthew talked a lot, quite a bit more than Peter ever has in her four years with him. Matthew is by far the youngest. His group ranges in age up to 10 years.

Last Sunday (September 7) we went to the farm. Cloud persisted all the way to Shellbrook, then the sun appeared brilliantly and made the early fall colours sparkle. We followed Papa’s dust plumes along Stump Lake and Blue Heron road and arrived at the Isaachsen’s in Canwood just as they were getting out of the truck. We ate, then went for a long walk around the village. We used Peter’s binoculars to see if anything was happening inside high school windows, the boys skipped on and off curbs, Matthew fell in oily dirt (getting grease on his pants doesn’t bother him much; he’s not even bothered that his mother is bothered.) We found the streets and alleyways curiously haphazard, short, with intriguing ins and outs, abrupt ends in odd places, like a private garden. Cheryl and I talked about the pros (the charm) and cons (everything else) of living in such a small place. We found a playground, but it wasn’t long before Cheryl decided she was wearing the wrong shoes. We returned to the house in time for the second meal. From as far off as the high school we saw Cheryl’s Mom holding Erik. Peter confirmed it through his binoculars. The drive home involved trading the boys back and forth with Cheryl’s parents. Matthew sang Twinkle with little flourishes. When Peter wasn’t discussing his impressions of what made people in the family similar and different, he was watching Papa and Mama in the truck through his binoculars.

Yesterday: We waited for Peter to finish with orchestra before shopping for new glasses and a baby seat. It is cool and cloudy. We’re not much encouraged to be outside. But we did go for a donut with Cheryl’s parents. This evening I took the boys to a Hilltop football game at Gordie Howe bowl. Peter fiddled with his gloves, getting the elastic sleeve just perfect, looking up now and then to see if the score had changed, finally taking the gloves off. Matthew was sitting, standing, wondering each time the crowd cheered if the game ‘was over, and when can we go to the next football game.’ We witnessed vagrancy at its most perfect. The thousand and more fans were up and down and around all night, to eat, stretch, visit, go to the bathroom, and eat some more. We left before the end of the third quarter.
