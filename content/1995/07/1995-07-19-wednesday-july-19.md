Not sure how we’re going to do Disneyland. We were told that the earlier we were there the better. So we were up at 5:50 and through the gates at 8. We had a short conversation while standing in line for tickets (the first of many over the next two days) with a local who had moved from New York who told us he had travelled to Alberta and BC, had loved it, and thought of moving to Calgary: no graffiti.

Our first lineup is the longest of all: Indiana Jones. The ‘vehicle’ needed to be ‘driven’. I sat behind the wheel. All the ups and downs, side to side, breaking, accelerating to evade rolling boulders and other extreme hazards, taking sharp turns in the pitch dark, and Erik is still young enough to believe I was the one in control. I was heroic. All the rest of the day were lines. Disney knows how to move many people quickly. This is our first experience following the system of folding the line on itself, back and forth, to create the illusion of swift motion towards the ticket booths. We saw movies in circular theatre, rode trams, railways, and roller coasters. Matthew is ill. He missed out on Space Mountain. The motion in the dark seemed too much for his disturbed GI. Sudden urgencies remove him and me from our place in lines, heading for the nearest bathroom, returning quickly enough to rejoin Cheryl, Peter and Erik before they’ve disappeared through the gate. We suspect he was infected with a bug in the water in Mexico while brushing his teeth. The entire Disneyland is a series of themes of an idealized, imagined, America. Critter Country, Frontierland, Tomorrowland. Parents show increasing fatigue over the day as the children get worn down by the energy and excitement: tears, children sleeping, anger. Erik does well but begins to fall behind by mid-afternoon. We are in and out of countless Disney stores. We stay for the fireworks and a fantastic light and water show, witnessing things as Mickey Mouse imagines them.
