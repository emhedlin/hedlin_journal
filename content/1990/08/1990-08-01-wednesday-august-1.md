We were up by 5:30 and in the van by 6. We carried Matthew and Erik still in their pjs. We planned to get to Twin Falls for breakfast. Just short of that, we pulled over to look into the river canyon. We couldn’t get near enough to the edge to get a real feel for distances. We threw a few stones in and took a couple of pictures. Mama stayed in the van with a magazine.

Breakfast at a Best Western. The best coffee so far.

We drove south to Wells Nevada through parched land, ashy-white soil, tumble-weed, sage, high hills at a distance from both sides of the highway rising to 4000 ft. A few small ranches, though nothing like in Montana. Peter thinks we’re in rattlesnake country. At Wells we had our first encounter with slot machines. A bank of them were in the gas station, being played by locals. We got on the I-80 and arrived in Elko for beef and barley soup at the Coffee Mug restaurant. It is attached to a motel and swimming pool. The further we get, the more stereotypical the places seem. Women with long platinum hair. We arrived in Winnemucca at 2. Papa had made motel reservations there These were cancelled.  Erik woke up and we had ice-cream instead. I felt some regret we haven’t the time to explore these places. It doesn’t take long to find out there isn’t much on either side of the main drag. Erik and Matthew sat with their cones at a little wooden table. Peter took offense when we suggested he sit with them.

The drive to Reno was 160 miles. Matthew sat on the red igloo cooler between Papa and me. While Papa slept he asked me about some of the jobs I had worked at, and the jobs I might have done if I wasn’t teaching. He would like to be an illustrator for kid’s books, or a police investigator, the kind that travels around the world. Eventually Papa woke up. Matthew moved onto his lap and counted dust devils. ‘Mini tornados lost and looking for their father,’ he said.

We arrived in Sparks-Reno by 5:30, followed a complicated route to the centre of Reno, found the Sundowner, parked in a massive parkade, went to our rooms on the 11th floor south towards Circus Circus and Harrah’s. Once settled, we walked to Circus Circus for supper. Which wasn’t nearly as straightforward as I expected. The place is monstrous beyond imagining. One walks through sea-sized rooms of slot machines, stunning with their light and sound. Pass through a maze of carnival and video games, always keeping a grip on Erik. We find a large area designed for circus acts. Ten-minutes long with a half hour in between. Trapeze, cannonball, sword juggling, dancing dogs, a circus announcer, and a two man band performed for the packed crowd kept back by a perimeter railing. After taking in two acts and .25 worth of shooting rifles for each boy, and poking each other in front of mirrors, we went for the smorgasbord. We found one room with three rows of buffet counters and separate rooms with tables. The décor was white with red stripes with a clattery ambience. After eating we returned to the circus, watched two more shows—one, actually, because the second, a motorcycle riding around inside a metal globe, was cancelled. Erik and Matthew climbed onto the retaining rail, enjoyed the dog act, and took another ‘shot’ at the rifle gallery. For Erik I sighted the gun and held it while Erik squeezed the trigger. On the way back to the Sundowner we strolled along the main avenue with ‘The Biggest Little Town in the World’ sign, and storefronts with flaring, sparkling neon signs, traffic flowing by on parade, people streaming in and out of the casinos. Eventually the boys were tired. Erik shows how tired he is by how hard he is to handle. We took them back to bed, opened the drapes of the wall-sized window, and watched the lights. Everyone was worn out by the 570 miles of road and went to bed at 10:30.
