Up at 7. I read awhile in bed. Erik woke up and joined me until Cheryl woke up and the day began. Cheryl’s parents suggested we go to Bally’s in Sparks for breakfast.

The parking lot and casino makes Saskatoon Inn look like a dollhouse. Even more slot machines here than in Circus Circus. The Patio Restaurant is lined up. While Mama keeps our place, we go by escalator to the sub-floor where we find large shops, a theatre, wedding chapel, golf shop, and bowling alley. We arrive at the restaurant just as Mama is being led to a table. While eating, Matthew draws a mountain scene. When we’re done, Cheryl, Mama and Peter go back to the sub-floor to shop. Matthew and I wander around the slots. We come across Papa while getting his shoes shined by a 44 year old grandmother. I win $2.50 with a dirty quarter. Cheryl returns with a $150 silk jacket. Her mom buys a matching skirt. We watch a woman working three machines, taking dollar coins from a tray. A floorwalker tells Matthew to leave. Nevada law doesn’t permit anyone under 21 within four feet of a slot.

We climb several thousand feet up a steep winding road to Virginia City, then go sharply down and around a curve. Cheryl won’t look out the window. There are houses on the summit connected to the main road by primitive trails. How does one get in and out when it rains and snows?

Virginia City is a replicated 19th century mining town with boardwalks, the Delta Saloon’s ‘suicide table’, the Bucket of Blood Saloon, a mansion, an opera house. Slot machines are everywhere. We ate a lunch of sandwiches and Sprite with (Mama’s opinion) too much ice.  We pass abandoned mine works on the way to Carson City. The road to Lake Tahoe is uphill, twisting, two-way, crowded with vehicles, much of it through Jeffrey, Sugar, and Ponderosa pines. Once at Lake Tahoe we turn into an avenue of cabins (an almost ironic term for places that are huge, professionally gardened, make one think of millions stashed in Swiss accounts), tree-lined avenues, curving streets, more professional landscaping, long bungalows with brick fences, some so high one has to wonder what they’re hiding, more casinos, and a large crowd on the street watching a boxing match, people streaming in every direction on foot, bikes, and a private airport full of ‘daddy’s’ jets. We take the road out of town on the way to Sacramento on a 6% grade. Mama’s terribly nervous. Cheryl is looking everywhere but out. Logging trucks and road construction slow the traffic. We cross bridges over ragged streams, pass by valley-cleft settlements. We’re passed by cars travelling high speeds. A campervan with two wheels sits stranded on a sharp curve.

Once out of the mountains, the lanes east and west are separated by boulevards of pink bougainvillea. Two lanes each way become three. Traffic is increasingly heavy. Lane-jockeys confuse things. It’s a jeep-jamboree. We reach Sacramento by 5.

We search for our Motel 6. We get too far down Howe Avenue and have to turn around. Most stunning, even for the summer season, is the wealth of vegetation, the flowering shrubs, coniferous varieties, and California sycamore. We take our rooms—two boys will sleep on the floor—and go for supper. Cheryl had read about a pizza place somewhere on J Street. We follow Folson Blvd past 67th. I check maps. Annabelle’s is on 200 J Street, West Sacramento, 65 blocks away. We come to old interesting homes at 40th Avenue, palm trees in every yard: the advantage of a year-round growing season. Finally we get to Annabelle’s, park the van, and can’t resist (some of us) looking in on every other restaurant. We follow a flight of stairs into an old building, its window propped up by a car jack. Once seated we learn that the unlimited pizza/pasta deal was only for lunch. We order the family special and a small #0 Cheese. After we’ve eaten, we head back onto the boardwalk, wandering from store to store: kites, books, gadgets. Peter and I walk in the back alley and listen to the sound of reggae coming from the other side of a stone fence. We meet three young men soliciting a crowd to hear three ‘great’ reggae bands. Many are sitting on the grass or standing by the wall, listening to this appealing, enlivening sound. Others stroll past or cross the main road and tracks towards more restaurants and a riverboat nightclub. We walk until dark, drive past Popeye’s Place with a long line of patrons onto J, and back to the motel. Matthew and Erik get the floor, and Peter the single bed. Cheryl and I read the Sacramento Bee until 11.
