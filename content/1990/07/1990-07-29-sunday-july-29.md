As arranged the previous night, Cheryl’s parents arrived in the van at 7 am.  The suitcases had been packed the night before.  When the boys woke (Peter claimed he hadn’t slept at all) they dressed. After a hodge-podge breakfast, dishes were washed, windows were checked, the water heater was turned down, the suitcases were packed.

Peter and Matthew sat in the back seat with their Archie comics, toys, games. Extended quiet prevails. Cheryl, Erik and Mama sit in the centre. Papa and I sit in the front, spelling each other off. Papa drove the first shift to Rosetown. I drove to Swift Current. We pulled up to the Horseshoe Motel at 10 for breakfast. We were shown to a large round table. Erik preferred standing by a cigarette machine to pull the knobs, hiding every time I came around the corner to see what he was up to. Matthew spent more time in the bathroom than at the table. Which is par for the course. When Cheryl took Erik to the Women’s bathroom, he protested. He declared it’s time he went to the Men’s room with his brothers. He finally joined us at the table. After eating his eggs and toast, Cheryl and I took him for a tour of the outdoors to burn off some steam. In time he complained that one leg was tired. Back at the restaurant, we found everyone raring to go.

The drive to the border was uneventful. Nothing very dramatic to look at. The US Customs official at Climax had wandered up the road visit with his Canadian counterpart. When he arrived he asked us almost conversationally what we were intending. We crossed over, pulled into the parking lot, and everyone went to the bathroom. We passed Turner, glanced off Harlem, went through Loma (the bridge across the Milk River), Chinook, then into Havre by 3 and up the hill to the Mall. Mama needed a pair of shoes. It would take only five minutes. It turned into 2 hours. We left at 5. Cheryl followed her mother from store to store. Cheryl found clothes at Herberger’s for a discount on a sale’s sale. Peter wandered with Papa, then joined me at a bench where I was keeping an eye on Matthew and Erik, who were climbing over each other in a flying saucer, then running back and forth between that and a motorcycle and a helicopter. I recalled how five years earlier, when Matthew was 2, a teenage girl stopped to admire him. She said, ‘I’ll wait for you’. Once the buying was don, we went back down to Havre for supper at the Four-Bs. We drove to Great Falls and checked into the Super 8 just off 10th Avenue, behind another mall. Hardly were we settled in our rooms and Cheryl’s parents were heading to the Target store. Peter and Matthew went to bed. Cheryl and I took Erik for a walk through the residential area across the street from the motel.
