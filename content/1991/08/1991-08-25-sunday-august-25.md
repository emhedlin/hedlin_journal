Peter, Matthew, Papa and I went to the driving range at Greenbrier Golf Course. It was Peter’s idea, although Cheryl and I had talked all summer about doing some golfing. Peter had actually wanted me to join him on the course, but I thought it better to limit my first experience to the driving range. Four events proved me right. First, after purchasing tokens for several buckets of balls I put one into the machine and watched the balls drop and bounce all over the concrete pad. Only then did I discover the empty buckets I was supposed to set in place to catch them. Second was the driver I used. It was Dad’s. The head was secured to the shaft with masking tape. Third was my first drive. The ball I struck went sideways, clearing the noses, just, of the men and women to my right. The fourth would wait for half an hour. In the meantime Matthew did well for his first time. Peter knows what he’s doing. His drives rise high, straight, and depending on the club travel between 75 and 150 yards. The four of us kept hitting until the first load of balls was exhausted. Papa went to get more but the machine was empty. A worker had gone onto the range to collect more so we assumed he’d take a while. Since everyone was wanting more, to kill time we went into the clubhouse, ordered coffee and ice-cream for the boys, sat on the deck, enjoyed the sun, talked about winter travel plans, and waited for the machine to be filled. A half-hour later we were back out. The fourth event was a grande finale: I took a full backswing, over-swung going forward, missed the ball, went off my feet and landed on my back. Some may have gotten a good laugh, but I didn’t look around to see. Even if the boys had kept quiet about it, the grass stains on my back were all Cheryl needed to know how things went. I will never again sit in the Willow’s restaurant and laugh at the first-timers. Comical as their swings may be, not one I’ve seen has ended up on his back.

We were invited to Mama's for supper. Her sister Judy was to be there. She had just come from the hospital where her husband Ronnie is just out of intensive care, being treated for severe burns for reasons no-one is quite clear about, but we assume was his ‘still’ exploding in his basement. We ate on the patio: barbecue roast, corn on the cob, carrot sticks, french bread and juice. We watched the 6 o’clock news to see that the Blue Jays had come from 6-2 behind to beat the Yankees. I had turned it off in the fifth inning. I’m quite sure that me watching my teams has a unconsoling effect on the athletes.

Peter and Matthew register at Lakeridge tomorrow. Matthew is clapping his hands with excitement. Peter is feeling more cautious. He won’t know until Tuesday what he is in for. Erik wanted to go to bed early tonight to bring tomorrow more quickly, because he’s going to Shirley’s in the morning and kindergarten at Buena Vista in the afternoon.

Last night we had joined Mom, Dad, Matthew, Cheryl, Diane and Ernie at Kinsmen Park for a picnic. Matthew and Cheryl go to Texas today. We may not see them for years if their visas to Nepal come through. The children were noisy and had a lot of fun. Every one of them went to the play village for a while, then returned to go on the train. When it turned out that its wheels had fallen off in the tunnel, they went on the merry-go-round. We left when it got dark and the mosquitoes were unmanageable. Better I suppose than the wasps hovering inside our glasses of orange juice earlier in the evening.
