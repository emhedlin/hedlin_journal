The summer is passing quickly. Cheryl's classes are over. She wrote her last final on Friday, a three hour open-book. The last two weeks have been long for her. She has worked hard and is anxious to begin her holiday.

But we have had a memorable summer. The boys have been busy with activities. Matthew took a week of soccer camp. Cheryl drove him, parked in Harvey's stall by the Phys-Ed building, left him with his instructors, and walked to the Biology building where her class is held. Peter is completing his White badge at the Lakewood civic centre. He likes his instructor. She is young but demanding. She told me that Peter's only area needing improvement is to reach farther ahead on his front crawl and he bring his hand up nearer his on his back crawl. The rest, his stride entry, head-up crawl, safety procedures, endurance are all very good. He did pass and is now ready to work on his Bronze Medallion. Matthew and Erik participated in the Olympic Way swim program at the university.   Matthew went for six weeks, Erik for four. Matthew was bumped up to Level 2 even though three years ago he didn't get his Level 1 because of ear infections. The instructor felt he would be out of place with four year olds. He did well in Level 2, was moved into Level 3, passed that in two weeks, moved into Level 4, and passed that in two weeks. He has a strong quick stroke. He has completed as much as Peter by the end of his Level 4, except for the butterfly, which they seem not to be teaching. He loves swimming, but he wants to get rid of the ear plugs that make it difficult to hear the instructor. His hair has that chlorine sheen. Erik began in Level 1. He too loves the water, but is careless because he doesn’t know enough to fear it. Most of the time he wears a life jacket, but he wants to put his head under the water. His best ‘stroke’ is the bob-and-breathe, which involves letting go of the side of the pool, sinking and then recovering to grab the side again. He looks over at Cheryl and me and smiles. But he makes Cheryl nervous. He goes about with a kick-board in depths well over his head. He is smaller than the others and falls behind. He seems not to care that the others are ahead, and twists and turns according to the patterns in his mind. He jump into the water and dogpaddles the four feet back to the side, though there is the danger that someone will get in the way. We aren’t sure how he’d react if that happened. Cheryl knows what I would do. I’d jump up and save him. After four weeks he is much improved in a lot of areas. He nearly passed the first level. We’ll wait to put him back in Olympic Way for a couple of years until he is bigger and ready to do the lengths that kids in Level 2 are required to do.
