School is out. I’m moving to Walter Murray next fall to teach Grades 9 and 12 Social Studies. Peter is promoted to Grade 1 with a very enthusiastic 2-half report. Cheryl’s part-time load will continue until February. When Shelley Loeffler goes off on maternity leave, Cheryl will go full-time. Matthew shows changes every day, speaking more and being livelier and goofier.

Thursday and Friday were spent at Waskesiu and Christopher Lake. We would have stayed longer at Christopher had we not been bumped from Adolph’s cabin by counsellors. We had arrived there by 2, Thursday afternoon, settled in, went for a walk along the beach, then drove past an unsigned bible camp at the north end of the lake onto a narrow trail through the woods to a trap-line. Peter and I spent a while in a canoe, then we waded with Cheryl and Matthew. At some point Peter went missing. Cheryl and I called but got no answer. We searched the beach, the Kinasao campground, the paths into the trees. A six year old in a forest by a lake. What could go wrong? We found him eventually. He had been hiding in the loft, listening, thinking, no doubt, that his trick was playing out very well. That evening we tried to get the boys to sleep in the same bed. It didn’t work. Next morning we drove to Waskesiu, had coffee at the café, and spent from 11 until 3:30 on the beach in the blazing sun. Peter and Matthew dug sand, played in the water, looked for minnows, floated on an air raft. I am reading Michael Hayden’s book about Aunt Hilda, So Much To Do, So Little Time, which I’ll review for an STF publication. Cheryl read Stewart’s The Wicked Day. We returned to Christopher, planning to come back on Saturday, then discovered we were losing our spot. We stopped at Fran’s Diner in MacDowell. The crops, the white picket fences, the surrounding forest, gives the place the look of the 1950s.

Besides enjoying the time off, I have a lot to do to be ready for the fall. Cheryl wants to repaint the picnic table. The Wallaces next door finished their portion of the fence, which means the yard is now fully enclosed and Matthew can have a free run. Peter played cello yesterday. We’re reading Stuart Little. Cheryl and her mom will take the boys to tomorrow’s Traveller’s Day parade.

Last Saturday was the final performance of Showboat. It was a great success. Over 5000 attended during its run. It improved with each night. The entire thing revolved around Cheryl’s performance as nun, groupie, off-stage chorus. She was clearly enjoying the whole thing, smiling and blinking her eyes like a child in the thrall of something. Last night we attended the closing event at the STF building, a dinner and dance.

Michael and Cathy had been in town from June 27 to July 3. They saw Showboat on Thursday. We went with them on Friday to the Tent ‘n Tavern.

My toe still hurts after kicking a wall during a barefoot soccer match in the front hall of Roland Michener in June.

The boys have joined the league of break-dancers. They do a tandem version of ‘the worm’. Peter flops around the room, and Matthew bows.

Peter is an increasingly assertive, even cheeky, commentator. He argues a lot, resists doing what he doesn’t want, messes up his room, eats only what he likes, and when he judges that we’ve exceeded our parental prerogative, he says we need to change our behaviour. He points out contradictions. The month-long ban from playing with his shotgun doesn’t apply to his water pistol. He won’t ride his bike so long as the pedal is injured, rides double with Papa (who can’t talk or laugh without the jujubes he’s eating falling into Peter’s hair). He told Grandma Skarsgard that the Ovation chocolates she gave him are actually cigarettes. He has gained enough farm experience to judge the condition of crops. While on the highway he remarked, ‘Oh, what a lovely rape field over there.’ And enough experience with life to judge how to die. ‘Dying clean,’ he said, ‘is far better than dying dirty, for when people are looking at you in the auditorium.’ He told Papa, ‘That’s a good job of ‘hedging’ you did around the yard.’

Today we went to Beaver Creek. The four trails are now fully developed. Matthew walked the entire length of a ¼ km path. The boys ran around in the outdoor theatre. We had supper at Venice’s in Sutherland.
