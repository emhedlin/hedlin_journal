The morning was spent shopping for goggles for Cheryl and Peter. It’s not easy to find the model that Cheryl says she thinks she’s sure she saw someone wearing. Peter bathed twice with his. Cheryl stayed home while I took the boys to George Ward. We swam together at noon for an hour. We’re tired, and had trouble pushing ourselves. Cheryl’s goggles worked fine.

Peter tried to nap. Once which I passed by his door to look in he closed his eyes. I asked why he wasn’t sleeping. He pretended to sleep. I waited. I his eyelids flutter. This is quite the game he plays. Cheryl and I tried to sleep. Matthew wasn’t feeling well and slept only a short time. We tried the tough approach, which means trying not to encourage him. Trying one thing usually becomes doing something else. With someone as stubborn as Matthew, this meant getting up and hoping everyone would want to go to bed a bit earlier this evening.

Cheryl arranged with her mother to have a perm after swimming. On the way over we stopped at Consumer Distributors to buy an earring set to replace the one she lost. Matthew walked on top of a retaining rail in front of the counter. He seemed to be feeling better. Peter was inciting him to scream. Another boy several months older than Matthew became interested in him. They touched hands and faces and explored each other with their 78 rpm voices. Cheryl’s parents were not at home. We put the boys to bed, read and put ourselves to bed by 10. We have decided to leave for Calgary to attend a Suzuki workshop on Saturday. That leaves tomorrow to get the house ready and the car packed. Erin Bingham was over. She had a bag of perishable food that Maureen wanted to get rid of before they left for the week. Cheryl told Erin we couldn’t take it because we also were going away. Erin didn’t understand and pressed the food on Peter, faithfully repeating her mother’s instructions, “Take it, we’re going on holidays.” Erin finally left with the food.
