We left with Michael and Cathy for Banff by 10. Peter sat in the back and led a conversation about births. He talks and questions even when the answers he gets have no particular focus or meaning. He’s a Pandora’s Box of inquiry. The day was warm and clear. We were there by 11:20, and parked on Banff Avenue. We shopped for food for Matthew, were greeted at Samo’s Broiler by a Grecian statue with a cardboard “Please Wait to be Seated” sign around its neck. A good meal but Matthew posed his usual challenges. Michael and Cathy went off to hike on Rundle’s lower slopes, and we drove by the Douglas Fir Chalets on Tunnel Mountain where we honeymooned to discover that the quaint cabins with flower boxes, varnished interior and fireplace had been replaced by A-frames. We found a viewing spot over the Bow River valley. Peter and I sat on a stone. Cheryl tried for a photo of Peter with his arm around my neck, but no more film. We visited the Luxton Indian Museum, and for $4 we spent 90 minutes with Peter asking how animals were killed, what the sun dance was about, and the Buffalo hunt, etc, and pulled Matthew off the fragile displays. Then it was a drive up Sulphur Mountain, back into Banff for ice-cream, and up Spray Avenue to the Bow Falls. Cheryl waited at the car with a sleeping Matthew, and Peter and I threw stones into the river. When Michael and Cathy returned, we went to the Golf Chalet, studied the dress regulations, and crossed the wooden bridge to the Banff Springs Hotel. Peter had punched a button on a Coke machine and got a free drink. We bypassed the outdoor and indoor pools, toured the lobby and mezzanine floors, viewed $1,000 rings, and watched people go by. Matthew was in a pack on my back. Peter ran about. We took an elevator to the top, peeked into open rooms on the 6th and 7th, listened to a tour-guide give information about the mushroom staircase and its handrails polished twice daily, and circled inside a medieval banquet room on a dimly lighted balcony floor. We left Banff by 5 in time for Cathy to see her parents after their visit with her brother in Traill. We ate the rest of yesterday’s Japanese noodles and then packed. The boys were dressed for bed when Cathy phoned inviting us over. We visited with her parents and drank tea for a half hour. Michael came home with us. The boys went to bed, Cheryl read, and Michael and I talked more about guitars.
